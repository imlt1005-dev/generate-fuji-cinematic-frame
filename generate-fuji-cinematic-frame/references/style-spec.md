# Film-Inspired Style Specification

## Reference-Derived Identity

The target visual identity is a quiet vertical film diary:

- a 9:16 black canvas containing two or three very wide photographic frames;
- individual travel frames near 1.9:1, with 2.25:1 reserved for a deliberately wider letterboxed look;
- generous black breathing room above and below the sequence;
- thin black gutters separating frames;
- ordinary people, vernacular architecture, water, weather, and small environmental details;
- visual continuity through place, color, time, or motif rather than repeated poses;
- subtitles used selectively as film dialogue or reflective narration;
- no gallery UI, page counts, close buttons, or other screenshot artifacts.

The canonical visual example is `reference-triptych-imlt1005-dev.jpg`. Inspect it for the intended black breathing room, equal panel rhythm, in-frame bilingual subtitles, and the progression from everyday place to human movement to waterside afterimage. The image is a reference only and must not be copied into unrelated user outputs.

## Color Directions

These are original, film-inspired working directions. They are not exact emulations of proprietary recipes.

### soft-eterna

Use for quiet people, interiors, water, overcast light, and reflective stories.

- slightly reduced saturation;
- gentle contrast with protected highlights;
- open but not gray shadows;
- subtly warm skin and neutral whites;
- blues lean slate rather than cyan;
- greens remain deep and natural;
- fine, restrained grain.

### documentary-chrome

Use for streets, shops, older neighborhoods, travel, and observational scenes.

- restrained saturation with clear local separation;
- modestly dense midtones;
- warm reds and brick without orange clipping;
- subdued blue and green;
- neutral skin;
- crisp structure without digital oversharpening.

### warm-negative

Use for sunlight, childhood, summer, domestic memory, and nostalgic movement.

- moderately stronger contrast;
- warm highlights and slightly cool shadows;
- lively but controlled reds and yellows;
- greens shift slightly olive;
- blacks remain textured;
- avoid blanket sepia.

### vivid-summer

Use for blue sky, clouds, mountains, old roofs, and bright water.

- clean blue sky and luminous white cloud;
- saturated but believable vegetation;
- warm stone and wood;
- controlled highlights;
- no HDR halos or electric cyan.

### monochrome-humanist

Use for portraits, architecture, solitude, rain, and memory.

- broad gray scale;
- solid but not crushed blacks;
- restrained highlight roll-off;
- fine grain concentrated in midtones and shadows;
- no artificial vintage scratches.

## Cinematography Rules

- Build the story from a location, an action, and a visual afterimage.
- Prefer 28–50 mm observational framing; use longer focal-length compression only for a narrative reason.
- Allow foreground obstruction, reflections, windows, doors, rails, trees, and other layers.
- Use one dominant light source with a plausible origin.
- Preserve environmental detail; do not default to shallow depth of field.
- Keep poses incidental and human. A frame should feel found, not staged for an advertisement.

## Texture Rules

- Use fine grain rather than large noise.
- Preserve skin and sky from excessive texture.
- Avoid simulated dust, torn film, borders, date stamps, and light leaks unless explicitly requested.
- Avoid extreme sharpening, clarity, HDR microcontrast, and waxy denoising.
